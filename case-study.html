01 / The brief

Bring employee records and everyday HR tasks into one dependable system.

People operations touch every part of a business.

JERP HRM brings core people workflows into one enterprise system. The design focus is a dependable daily workspace: make the state of work easy to scan, give common actions a clear home, and keep employee information connected to the tasks around it.

02 / Challenges & response

Three design tensions shape a clearer, more connected HR experience.

Turn workflow complexity into clear next steps.

01 / Visibility

Requests and follow-ups can compete for attention across a busy HR workspace.

Important work is easy to miss.

Design response

Give pending work a shared overview with clear status, useful context, and an obvious next action.

02 / Continuity

Everyday processes rely on employee information, but can feel disconnected when those details are hard to reach.

People data and tasks are connected.

Design response

Keep employee records and related workflows within a coherent information structure, with predictable ways to move between them.

03 / Consistency

Leave, attendance, and employee updates have different rules, while still needing to feel like one product.

Each process has its own states.

Design response

Use shared patterns for status, navigation, and actions, then adapt the details to each workflow’s needs.

04 / Design priorities

Three principles for making everyday HR work clearer and easier to move through.

Designing for clearer everyday decisions.

01 / ORIENT

Start with the work at hand.

Bring pending requests and useful team context into a single overview, so the next action is easier to find.

02 / CONNECT

Keep people and processes in view.

Organize employee records alongside the recurring workflows that depend on them, instead of treating each screen as a separate destination.

03 / CLARIFY

Make each step feel predictable.

Use consistent language, visible status, and clear action hierarchy to reduce uncertainty across everyday HR tasks.

05 / How I approach it

A structured path from understanding workflows to refining the interface.

A connected system,
not a stack of screens.

Good workflow UX begins by understanding the relationships between roles, information, and decisions. From there, I shape a clear structure, test the important states, and carry the design through to responsive, accessible interface patterns.
